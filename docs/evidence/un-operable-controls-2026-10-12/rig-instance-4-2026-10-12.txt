Instance 4 over HTTP — the Request dead end, measured on a scratch instance — 2026-10-12

Scratch instance of origin/main 1dae076 (worktree, separate DB), bound to port 4720 with nothing else
touched: it was seeded, probed and torn down while the live product on 3000 answered 200 before and after.
The client was never opened — every line below is a raw request and the server's own response body.

What it shows, in one line each:
  * every seeded demo case carries SEVEN employee-grade items, including all three the notice gives no
    basis to (education_certificate, experience_certificate, emergency_contact) — so the dead end is
    present on the shipped demo, not only in a case a user creates.
  * a case opened through the product's own POST /api/preboarding/cases gets the same seven (201).
  * Request on a no-basis item: the status call answers 200 and the item becomes 'requested'.
  * Mark received: the same endpoint refuses 'received' with 409 and names the counsel question
    (Q1 for both certificates, Q5 for the emergency contact). The item stays 'requested'.
  * the roll-up does not move while the item is stranded: items_outstanding rises to 28 with the new
    case and stays 28 through all three stranded requests — the item counts against the case and no
    status the product accepts can clear it. That is the dead end, and it is permanent.

Reproduction: seed a scratch instance; log in; POST /api/preboarding/cases with an accepted offer;
take any no-basis item_key from GET /api/preboarding/cases/:id/checklist and POST its status
'requested' (200), then 'received' (409).

One correction to my own method, recorded here rather than quietly dropped: the first probe located
its item array by searching the payload recursively, and on two cases it landed on a shorter nested
array instead of the checklist's own 'items' field — which made those cases look like they carried
four items. The transcript below reads the documented top-level 'items' field and filters
track='employee'. The earlier reading was an artifact of the probe, not a property of the product.
