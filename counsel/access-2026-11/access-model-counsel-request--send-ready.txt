Access to personnel records — three questions: confirmation request

From: Antum People, an early-stage HR software product (legal entity not yet registered). Contact: Ahmad Aljairoudi, Dubai, UAE.

Our product holds two kinds of personnel record. One is the employee record: the company's own staff, with salary, department, start and end dates. The other is a pre-boarding case: a candidate's name, an email address, the role and department they are being hired into, the reporting line, the start date, and the documents and steps we chase before the first day. Access is by role — a person signs in with one role, and a role reaches a stated set of records and no further; where a role reaches nothing, the request is refused and the caller is told nothing about what exists. Three cells in that model we cannot settle from our own materials, and until they are settled we refuse them rather than choose. We are asking what the law permits or requires, not for a design opinion; please confirm or correct each point below, with the article and the Arabic wording where it matters.

1. Aggregates that could identify a person. We serve a dashboard of company-wide figures to HR, to finance, and to a read-only compliance role: headcount, payroll cost, accrued end-of-service liability, an average time-to-productivity per department, and a retention percentage per half-year start cohort. We publish no cohort's size and we apply no minimum group size: a department's average, or a cohort's percentage, is computed and shown however few people it covers. A manager of a team is refused the dashboard altogether, and we have marked that refusal as waiting on your answer rather than guessing a threshold. Is there a minimum group size below which an aggregate of personnel data should not be shown — and does the answer differ when the reader is the manager of the people inside the group, or when the group is a single department?

2. A candidate's contact details, before the start date. A pre-boarding case carries the candidate's name and an email address, the role and department they are joining, the reporting line and the start date. We intend the manager of that reporting line to be able to read their own line's pre-boarding cases — the people they are about to manage, before day one. We do not serve that today: nothing in our data yet says which case belongs to which person, and this question is unanswered, so the manager's access is refused. Is a candidate's name and contact details personal data that may be shown to the manager of the role before the candidate starts, and if so on what basis? Does it matter that at that point the candidate has no employment relationship with us yet?

3. What the person sees of their own pre-boarding record. For each case we keep two tracks: the employee track — the documents and steps asked of the hire — and a workspace track, the internal provisioning steps, where each line names the function responsible for it (IT, Admin, HR or the manager), its due date, and who last moved it. We have ruled that a person may read their own pre-boarding record — their own items, and the notice as it was shown to them — read-only and limited to the fields asked of them; we do not serve it yet, and this one question decides what it must contain. Must the person's own view include the internal working record — the responsible functions, the due dates and the names of the colleagues who moved each line — or may we show them their own items and the notice, and keep the internal working record internal? We are not asking whether they may see their own data; that is not in doubt. We are asking what of the internal record must be shown with it.

Until you confirm, we will not serve a manager a pre-boarding case, we will not build the person's own view with the internal working record on it, and we will not describe this access model as "compliant".

Please reply with (1) your confirmation or correction on each point with pin-cites, and (2) your fee and turnaround.
