UAE PDPL — privacy notice and lawful basis: confirmation request

From: Antum People, an early-stage HR software product (legal entity not yet registered). Contact: Ahmad Aljairoudi, Dubai, UAE.

Our product is built for HR teams in the UAE. Before a hire's first day it asks for a small set of documents and shows the hire a privacy notice, and it records that the notice was shown — per document, with the lawful basis and the notice version, and with the name of the person who showed it. We want that notice and that record to state only what the law requires. There are five points we cannot settle from our own materials. Please confirm or correct each, with the article and the Arabic wording where it matters.

1. Notice content. Our notice covers: controller identity, purposes of processing, categories of personal data, data-subject rights, retention periods, and cross-border transfers. Are those the elements a UAE privacy notice must contain, and is that list complete — or does the PDPL require something further? Please cite the article. Related, because it decides what we may collect: our notice currently has no purpose row covering qualification certificates, so we do not collect them at pre-boarding. Our category list does state Background & Onboarding Data — including qualifications and certifications — while the purpose table has no matching row, so the notice names a collection we do not perform. Should the category row stand, and what purpose and lawful basis should the matching purpose row state — or should the category row drop what we do not collect?

2. Arabic. Our notice is published in English and Arabic. Must a UAE privacy notice be in Arabic — and if so, must the Arabic be a certified legal translation, or is an in-house translation acceptable?

3. The instrument. For the documents whose lawful basis is performance of the employment contract or a legal obligation (passport, entry permit, Emirates ID, and payroll bank details), we do not ask the hire for consent. Instead we record that the notice was shown, per document, with the basis, the notice version, and the person who showed it. Is that sufficient to discharge the duty to inform for those documents, and is consent ever required for them? And where the hire cannot yet hold an account in our system, is a record kept by the employer on the hire's behalf acceptable?

   Our paper version of the notice is not consistent with that. Its final page asks the hire to sign and carries two separate lines: "I confirm I have read and understood this Privacy Notice", and "I consent to the processing of my personal data as described, including [sensitive categories, where applicable]", with a signature and a field labelled "Consent Version". Our software records that the notice was shown; the paper form collects consent. Which is the right instrument for the paper form? If a consent line should stay, for which categories must it be given — and what must the wording and the version field say, given that what we version is the notice and not a consent document?

4. Emergency contact. Our checklist carries an emergency contact — a third person's personal data, and a person who has no relationship with us at the time. We do not currently record it: the notice assigns no basis, so it stays out of the product until you answer. Our category list does state emergency contact details under Contact Data, while the purpose table assigns it nothing. Whose personal data is it, what lawful basis would permit collecting it before the first day, and who is entitled to give that basis: the hire, or the contact?

5. Retention. Our notice states a minimum of two years post-termination for employment and payroll records. Does that floor hold under UAE law, and does a retention floor attach specifically to the acknowledgement record itself — the record that the notice was shown, carrying its version and basis?

Until you confirm, we will not describe our notice or our acknowledgement process as "compliant", the article numbers in our own privacy notice stay unconfirmed until you answer — that notice already flags them itself, as drawn from our compliance reference set and pending official translations — and we will not collect the two qualification certificates or the emergency contact — those stay out of the product until you answer.

Appendix A carries the notice's own citations. The notice states fifteen things of its own about the law — the PDPL articles it names, the conditions it applies, one rights-response period, and the DIFC/ADGM carve-out — and we cannot source any of them from our own materials; none of the five points above puts them in question. Appendix A lists them one per row, with the line of the notice that carries each, and the ask for each row. **We assert no article number of our own**: every number in that table is the notice's, quoted, not ours.

Please reply with (1) your confirmation or correction on each point with pin-cites, and (2) your fee and turnaround.
